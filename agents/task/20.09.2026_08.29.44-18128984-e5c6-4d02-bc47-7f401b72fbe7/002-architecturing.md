## Portable invocation and persistence contract

Every invocation receives role instructions, unique invocation ID, explicit allowed input paths and content identities, one previously absent output path, write restrictions, and completion criteria. A role may have several invocations, never several implicit output paths. Artifacts are immutable after publication. A failed invocation's path is never reused. Exactly-once means one producer per allocated artifact instance, not one file per role for the entire task.

The Orchestrator allocates task-global, three-digit `NNN` monotonically before dispatch. Allocation order, not concurrent completion time, determines filename order. Gaps after failure are valid. At 999, stop with a process-failure escalation; do not wrap, renumber, or overwrite. Retain the current UTC task-ID format and historical no-rename rule. Use a configured `task_root`; this repository binds it to `agents/task`. Portable examples may use `<task_root>/<TASK_ID>` but must not require the project layout.

Council identity has two independent counters: `bBBB` identifies a frozen brief/run and increases after restarting with new material; `rRRR` identifies a reviewed plan revision within that brief, starting at 001. One round means one candidate plan plus its complete review set. The positive `iteration_limit` limits reviewed plan revisions, not brief IDs or tool calls. A new brief does not reset global step numbers or the outer workflow cycle budget.

Define `STEP_NUMBER_FORMAT` explicitly to keep existing references valid. Ordinary suffixes are `planning`, `coding`, `verification`, `validating`, and `orchestrator`. Historical `architecturing` remains recognized for grandfathered tasks only. Council suffixes, all preceded by the unique `NNN-`, are:

- `council-bBBB-brief`
- `council-bBBB-invocation-<id>` (Orchestrator's pre-dispatch contract)
- `council-bBBB-receipt-<id>` (Orchestrator's post-dispatch integrity evidence)
- `council-bBBB-proposal-<specialty>`
- `council-bBBB-rRRR-plan`
- `council-bBBB-rRRR-issues-before`
- `council-bBBB-rRRR-review-<specialty>`
- `council-bBBB-rRRR-issues-after`

- `council-bBBB-rRRR-final`
- `council-bBBB-terminal-evidence`
- `council-bBBB-gate`

`<id>` and `<specialty>` are lowercase alphanumeric/hyphen identifiers from the brief; defaults are architect, programmer, security, designer. Additional specialties require a complete role contract, capability requirement, and applicability rationale in configuration copied into the brief. Omitted defaults require evidence-based not-applicable rationales. At least one technical specialist is required. Run contracts name finite invocation timeout/completion behavior; do not prescribe a vendor-specific timer.

### Brief

New material facts, requirement changes, outside research results used substantively, changed baseline files, or changed membership require a new brief and fresh blind proposals. Analysis derived solely from existing inputs does not.

### Invocation and receipt

Invocation records contain the exact allow-list, role-instruction identities, input hashes, output allocation, forbidden input categories, context-isolation mechanism, write scope, completion criteria and finite timeout. Every blind specialist receives identical task/evidence inputs and the same complete portable role-instruction bundle; only assigned role, invocation ID, and output path differ. Receipt records reference the invocation and capture observed reads/context supplied, output hash, allowed/forbidden-flow checks, isolation evidence, before/after repository change inventory, and pass/fail. Self-attestation alone is insufficient where the adapter cannot prevent or detect inherited/peer context. Evidence must be durably summarized in task artifacts so the audit does not require access to hidden session history. Never persist private credentials or raw confidential execution logs.

### Proposal

Required sections: assumptions; observed evidence separated from inference; recommendation; credible alternatives and reasons; boundaries/files/modules/symbols/data flows/invariants; API/data/auth/migration/UI/rollout/rollback effects; risks; tests mapped to criteria; blockers; confidence and basis; excluded scope. A proposer does not vote, implement, inspect peer outputs, or decide final design.

### Plan revision

Required sections: source/provenance references; change summary from previous revision; requirements and acceptance mapping; chosen design and evidence (or explicitly unresolved alternatives in the initial collation); rejected alternatives and reasons; file/symbol inventory; ordered implementation slices; API/data/auth/migration/UI/rollout/rollback effects including justified N/A; test specifications mapped to every change and acceptance criterion; README updates; assumptions/confidence/remaining questions; dissent register with every material issue and proposed disposition; explicit Coding handoff and scope. The initial facilitator collation may have unresolved alternatives and a blocked handoff. A consent-eligible plan must be complete and actionable.

Delimit the implementation payload with exactly one line `<!-- council-plan:start -->` and one later line `<!-- council-plan:end -->`. Both plan and final use these delimiters. Identity is SHA-256 over exact UTF-8 bytes strictly between those lines, including original line endings and trailing newlines; no reformatting, normalization, or trimming. The payload must not embed its own hash, final artifact path, or future consent references. Store payload hash in the artifact envelope after generating the payload and before publication; subsequent records independently recompute it. Also checksum each complete immutable artifact when referenced by another artifact. Metadata cannot add implementation directions outside the accepted payload.

### Issue snapshots before and after review

Each snapshot contains plan path/payload hash, phase (`before` or `after`), source artifact identities, prior snapshot, and complete cumulative issue list. Each issue has stable issue ID, all originating roles/artifacts, requirement/invariant, failure mode, evidence, mitigation or precise question, materiality/blocking claim, status, rationale, and status-authority references. Valid statuses: `OPEN`, `PROPOSED_RESOLVED`, `RESOLVED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `NON_BLOCKING_NOTE`. Deduplication retains all originating claims and identities; never delete a record.

The before snapshot imports all proposals (r001) or previous after snapshot plus remands and revision rationale. The after snapshot imports every current review and preserves newly raised issues. The facilitator copies/attributes statuses; it does not determine technical validity. `PROPOSED_RESOLVED` is still blocking until the originating required role(s) explicitly accept the mitigation or withdraw with evidence in current reviews/consent and the complete required review set accepts the plan. Withdrawal without addressing a supported invariant violation cannot close it. Any disputed invalidity classification remains unresolved; seek evidence/remand or escalate. The Orchestrator verifies evidence and completeness, never substitutes its technical preference.

### Review

Required fields: specialty, exact plan path and payload hash, before-ledger path/hash, relevant source references, agreements, conflicts, tests/acceptance assessment, issue-by-issue dispositions, new issues, and verdict `AGREE`, `AGREE_WITH_NOTES`, or `OBJECT`. A blocking objection requires the four fields from the issue: violated requirement/invariant; concrete failure; supporting evidence; feasible mitigation or precise operator question. `AGREE_WITH_NOTES` cannot hide a required correction or unresolved blocker. Conditional consent such as “agree after changing X” is `OBJECT`. Preferences are recorded as nonblocking notes; unsupported objections are returned for clarification and cannot count as valid consent or be overruled as agreement. If the required response remains malformed or withholds valid consent, terminate PROCESS_FAILURE at the finite bound, not CONSENSUS.

### Remand

Include targeted question/claim, issue IDs, assigned specialty, permitted frozen inputs, evidence/analysis, proposed resolution, and remaining unknowns. Remands produce evidence only, not changed plans or consent. Budget is at most one targeted remand per required specialty per reviewed round in the project default; no unbounded serial remands. Remands that discover new material input trigger a fresh brief. New plan text always moves to the next rRRR and complete reviews.

### Consent

Each required specialty writes an explicit final `AGREE` or `AGREE_WITH_NOTES` against the same plan path/payload hash, after-ledger path/hash, complete matching review set, accepted issue dispositions and notes. Consent is requested only after all current reviews exist and all are accepting with no open blocker. Refusal/new issue does not vanish: use a new revision and full review if budget remains, or classify a terminal non-consensus outcome. Missing/invalid consent is PROCESS_FAILURE. No silence, model identity, headcount, or facilitator assurance supplies consent.

### Final, terminal evidence, and gate

The fresh chair writes the final artifact only after the Orchestrator's pre-seal check confirms the complete matching consent set. The final envelope names accepted plan and hash, all matching consent references, and its own writer/output path; its delimited payload is copied byte-for-byte from the accepted plan. No editorial cleanup is allowed in the payload. A requested intentional change requires a new revision/full review/consent within budget; an observed unexplained mismatch fails integrity and blocks the gate.

The facilitator's terminal-evidence artifact contains manifest of required/observed artifacts and identities, reviewed round count and remand counts, required roles, consent references, unresolved issue IDs, agreed sections, positions, evidence, attempted resolutions, precise operator question/decision if needed, and proposed terminal classification. The facilitator records evidence but cannot declare workflow state.

The Orchestrator writes the gate artifact independently verifying schema completeness, frozen input identity, allowed/forbidden information flow and receipts, unique writers/paths/logical slots, iteration/remand bounds, all issue dispositions, complete same-revision reviews/consent, plan/final payload equality, actionable test-mapped contract, and downstream scope. It records PASS or FAIL for each check with evidence, terminal state and rationale, and Coding handoff only for verified CONSENSUS. A missing facilitator record remains PROCESS_FAILURE; root may create a failure gate/escalation recording the omission but may not fabricate the missing role artifact.

## Exact convergence sequence and termination

1. Planning supplies facts and resolves requirements; Orchestrator freezes b001 and invocation allocations with identical blind evidence.
2. Required specialists produce blind proposals. Execute in isolated contexts, sequentially or in parallel only when the adapter guarantees isolation. A normal fork that includes peer artifacts is forbidden. A fresh chair has not authored a first-round proposal in the active run.
3. Facilitator mechanically collates r001 plan, then separately writes its before ledger. Conflicting alternatives stay attributed and unresolved; facilitator does not synthesize a technical choice.
4. All required specialists independently review the exact same plan/before-ledger pair. Peer current-round reviews are not inputs to these invocations. This stronger bounded-review rule avoids order-dependent answers. Once every review exists, facilitator writes the after ledger.
5. If unanimous acceptance and no blocker are established, request all specialists' explicit consent to that exact plan/after-ledger/review set. Otherwise collect bounded remands if useful; a fresh chair produces r002 with evidence-driven reconciliation; facilitator writes the new before ledger; repeat the entire review set. Later revisions may use the same chair role but never turn it into a technical vote.
6. Before starting r(RRR+1), verify it is within the frozen iteration limit. On the last permitted round, complete its review/evidence accounting; never skip reviews, demand unconditional acceptance, or extend the limit silently. No additional review round is permitted for a late seal change after exhaustion.
7. After complete consent, chair seals the exact payload, facilitator records terminal evidence, and root independently verifies/writes gate. Coding reads the named final plan, gate, task requirements, and implementation evidence; it need not reconstruct the debate.

Terminal precedence: invalid/missing artifacts, isolation, or contradictory protocol records → PROCESS_FAILURE; otherwise unanimous acceptance, no blocker and verified identical actionable final → CONSENSUS; otherwise a precise missing fact/operator decision needed to evaluate or reconcile a blocker → NEEDS_INFORMATION; otherwise incompatible supported constraints/objections after permitted rounds → IRRECONCILABLE. Valid objections do not become process failures just because no preferred design won. Unresolved runs include the complete escalation package. All non-consensus states prohibit Coding. Restart needs a new frozen brief; material changes restart proposals and never rewrite old artifacts. Outer workflow cycles remain bounded separately.
